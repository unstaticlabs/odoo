# Write clearly and concisely

- Identify the reader, purpose, required terminology, voice, and factual claims.
  Preserve meaningful uncertainty; concision must not create false confidence.
- Lead with the result or required action. Use concrete subjects and strong verbs;
  prefer active voice when the actor matters.
- Keep one idea per paragraph and related words together. Use parallel structure
  for comparable ideas and headings only when they improve scanning.
- Replace vague references and nominalizations with the actual actor, action,
  object, condition, or measure.
- Remove throat-clearing, repetition, filler, puffery, empty transitions,
  unearned intensifiers, and decorative formatting.
- Prefer the shortest wording that remains accurate, natural, and faithful to
  the author's voice. Report substantive meaning changes or unresolved ambiguity.
